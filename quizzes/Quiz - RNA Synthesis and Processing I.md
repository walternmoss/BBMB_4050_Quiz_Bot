Topic: RNA Synthesis and Processing I
Quiz Questions

Section 1: Conceptual Practice Questions

Question 1:
Describe the molecular mechanism by which the prokaryotic RNA polymerase holoenzyme identifies and initiates transcription at a specific DNA promoter sequence, detailing the roles of its components and the DNA elements involved.

Question 2:
Compare and contrast the physical forces and molecular interactions that lead to the dissociation of the RNA polymerase from the DNA template during Rho-dependent versus Rho-independent transcription termination.

Question 3:
Explain how the dynamic association and dissociation of the sigma factor with the RNA polymerase core enzyme acts as a critical regulatory switch during the transition from transcription initiation to elongation in prokaryotes.

Question 4:
A novel antibiotic is discovered that binds directly to the DNA template and prevents the formation of the transcription bubble. Based on the mechanisms described, would this antibiotic more closely resemble Actinomycin D or Rifampicin in its primary mode of action, and why?

Question 5:
Explain how the distinct mechanisms of action of Rifampicin and Actinomycin D, as described, allow for their specific clinical applications in treating bacterial infections (tuberculosis) and cancer, respectively.

Section 2: Answer Key & Mechanistic Explanations

Question 1
Core Principle: The RNA polymerase holoenzyme, guided by the sigma factor, recognizes specific DNA promoter sequences to initiate transcription by unwinding the DNA.
Target Answer: Transcription initiation in prokaryotes begins when the RNA polymerase holoenzyme, composed of the core enzyme and the sigma factor, binds to specific DNA promoter sequences. The sigma factor is crucial for recognizing these promoter elements, specifically the -10 (TATA box) and -35 elements upstream of the transcription start site. Upon binding, the holoenzyme unwinds the DNA double helix to form a transcription bubble, making the DNA template accessible for RNA synthesis. This precise recognition and unwinding by the holoenzyme are essential steps for the accurate and regulated initiation of RNA synthesis.

Question 2
Core Principle: Rho-dependent and Rho-independent termination mechanisms both lead to RNA polymerase dissociation but employ different molecular interactions to destabilize the RNA-DNA hybrid.
Target Answer: In Rho-dependent termination, the Rho protein binds to the nascent RNA transcript and utilizes its helicase activity to unwind the RNA-DNA hybrid within the transcription bubble, leading to the dissociation of the RNA polymerase from the DNA template. In contrast, Rho-independent termination relies on specific RNA sequences that form a stable hairpin loop structure in the nascent RNA transcript. This hairpin loop, followed by a poly-U tail, destabilizes the relatively weak A-U base pairing between the RNA transcript and the DNA template, causing the RNA polymerase to pause and subsequently dissociate from the DNA. Both mechanisms ultimately disrupt the RNA-DNA interaction, but Rho-dependent termination involves an active protein unwinding, while Rho-independent termination relies on intrinsic RNA secondary structure and weak base pairing.

Question 3
Core Principle: The sigma factor's transient association with the core enzyme directs promoter binding and initiation, while its dissociation enables processive elongation.
Target Answer: The sigma factor acts as a critical regulatory switch by guiding the RNA polymerase core enzyme to specific promoter sequences for initiation. It facilitates the binding of the holoenzyme to the -10 and -35 elements and the subsequent unwinding of the DNA to form the transcription bubble. Once a short RNA transcript has been synthesized and the RNA polymerase transitions from initiation to elongation, the sigma factor dissociates from the core enzyme. This dissociation is crucial because it allows the core enzyme to become highly processive, moving along the DNA template to synthesize a long RNA molecule without being hindered by the sigma factor's promoter-binding affinity. Thus, the sigma factor's dynamic association ensures accurate initiation, and its dissociation enables efficient elongation.

Question 4
Core Principle: The hypothetical antibiotic's mechanism of binding to DNA to prevent transcription bubble formation aligns with Actinomycin D's interaction with the DNA template.
Target Answer: This novel antibiotic would more closely resemble Actinomycin D. Actinomycin D inhibits RNA elongation by intercalating into the DNA template. While its stated effect is on elongation, its primary mode of action involves direct binding to and interference with the DNA structure. Rifampicin, on the other hand, targets the bacterial RNA polymerase beta subunit to prevent initiation, meaning it acts on the enzyme itself, not directly on the DNA template. Since the hypothetical antibiotic binds directly to the DNA template to prevent transcription bubble formation, its mechanism of interacting with the DNA itself is analogous to Actinomycin D's DNA intercalation, even if the specific stage of inhibition (initiation vs. elongation) differs.

Question 5
Core Principle: The selective targeting of bacterial RNA polymerase by Rifampicin and the general DNA intercalation by Actinomycin D dictate their distinct clinical uses.
Target Answer: Rifampicin is used to treat tuberculosis because it specifically targets the bacterial RNA polymerase beta subunit, preventing transcription initiation in bacterial cells. This specificity allows it to selectively inhibit bacterial RNA synthesis without significantly affecting human RNA polymerase, making it an effective antibacterial agent. Actinomycin D, in contrast, inhibits RNA elongation by intercalating into DNA. This mechanism is less specific, as it interferes with DNA-dependent RNA synthesis in both bacterial and eukaryotic cells. Its broad inhibitory effect on transcription in rapidly dividing cells, including cancer cells, makes it useful in cancer chemotherapy, despite its potential for toxicity to healthy human cells.
