Section 2: Answer Key & Mechanistic Explanations

Question 1
Core Principle: Light absorption causes a specific isomerization of retinal, leading to a conformational change in rhodopsin that initiates the visual signal transduction cascade.
Target Answer: Upon light absorption, the chromophore cis-retinal, which is covalently linked to the opsin protein in rhodopsin, undergoes photoisomerization to its trans form, trans-retinal. This change in retinal's conformation induces a significant conformational change in the rhodopsin protein itself. This altered conformation of rhodopsin is the activated state, which then becomes capable of interacting with and activating the G protein transducin, thereby initiating the downstream signaling cascade for vision.

Question 2
Core Principle: Vision involves hyperpolarization due to cation channel closure, while olfaction involves depolarization due to cation channel opening and ion influx.
Target Answer: In vision, light absorption leads to a decrease in cyclic GMP (cGMP) concentration. This decrease causes cGMP-gated cation channels in the photoreceptor cell membrane to close. The closure of these channels reduces the influx of cations, leading to hyperpolarization of the photoreceptor cell membrane. In contrast, in olfaction, odorant binding activates a G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR), which triggers the G protein Golf to stimulate adenylyl cyclase. This increases cyclic AMP (cAMP) concentration, which then opens cyclic nucleotide-gated cation channels. The opening of these channels allows an influx of Na+ and Ca2+ ions into the olfactory receptor neuron, resulting in depolarization and the generation of an action potential.

Question 3
Core Principle: Signal amplification in vision occurs through a G protein cascade, and recovery involves biochemical regeneration of the active chromophore and receptor.
Target Answer: Signal amplification in the photoinduction cascade begins when activated rhodopsin, following light-induced conformational change, activates multiple molecules of the G protein transducin. Each activated transducin then stimulates phosphodiesterase (PDE), an enzyme that rapidly hydrolyzes numerous molecules of cyclic GMP (cGMP). This enzymatic activity by PDE, stimulated by multiple transducin molecules, leads to a significant decrease in cGMP concentration, amplifying the initial light signal. For recovery, biochemical processes are essential to restore rhodopsin to its light-sensitive state and regenerate cis-retinal, allowing the photoreceptor cell to be ready for subsequent light detection.

Question 4
Core Principle: Capsaicin activates the TRPV1 channel, causing cation influx and depolarization, which is perceived as heat or burning.
Target Answer: Capsaicin, the active component of chili peppers, binds directly to the Transient receptor vanilloid type-1 (TRPV1) channel. TRPV1 is a nonselective cation channel. Upon capsaicin binding, the channel opens, allowing a rapid influx of both Ca2+ and Na+ ions into the cell. This influx of positive charges leads to depolarization of the cell membrane. Since TRPV1 is also naturally activated by high temperatures and low pH, the depolarization caused by capsaicin binding mimics the electrical signal generated by actual heat, leading to the sensation of heat or burning.

Question 5
Core Principle: Salty taste is detected by direct ion channel activity, while bitter taste involves GPCRs and a G protein signaling cascade.
Target Answer: The gustatory system employs fundamentally different initial transduction mechanisms for detecting 'salty' versus 'bitter' tastes. For 'salty' taste, the primary mechanism involves the direct detection of Na+ ions. These Na+ ions flow through amiloride-sensitive sodium channels (ENaC) located on the taste receptor cells. This direct influx of Na+ ions leads to depolarization of the cell membrane. In contrast, 'bitter' taste detection is mediated by G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) belonging to the T2R family. When bitter compounds bind to these T2R GPCRs, they activate the G protein gustducin. This activation of gustducin leads to an increase in intracellular Ca2+ and subsequent activation of TRPM5 channels, ultimately resulting in depolarization and neurotransmitter release.
