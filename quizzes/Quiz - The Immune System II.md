Topic: The Immune System II
Quiz Questions

Section 1: Conceptual Practice Questions

Question 1:
Describe the precise molecular interactions and components involved when a T cell recognizes an antigen presented on an MHC molecule, detailing the roles of the TCR, MHC, and associated co-receptors.

Question 2:
Upon successful recognition of a peptide-MHC complex by a T cell, a critical intracellular signaling cascade is initiated. Explain the immediate molecular event that triggers this cascade, identifying the key protein domains and enzyme involved.

Question 3:
The adaptive immune system employs distinct T cell populations to address different types of threats. Mechanistically differentiate how Cytotoxic T Lymphocytes (CTLs) and Helper T Lymphocytes (Th cells) recognize their target cells and subsequently execute their specific effector functions.

Question 4:
Consider a scenario where a mutation in the Lck kinase renders it completely inactive. Predict and explain the direct consequence of this mutation on T cell activation and subsequent cellular immune responses, referencing the molecular steps outlined in the study guide.

Question 5:
Cellular immunity plays a crucial role in maintaining host health. Explain how the mechanisms of T cell recognition and effector function contribute to both cancer surveillance and the pathogenesis of autoimmune diseases, drawing specific connections to the concepts of self-tolerance.

Section 2: Answer Key & Mechanistic Explanations

Question 1
Core Principle: T cell recognition involves a multi-component molecular complex formed between the T cell receptor, MHC molecule, and specific co-receptors.
Target Answer: T cell recognition begins when the T cell receptor (TCR) on the T cell surface binds specifically to a peptide-MHC complex displayed on an antigen-presenting cell (APC) or target cell. The TCR recognizes the combined structure of the peptide antigen nestled within the MHC molecule. Simultaneously, co-receptors stabilize this interaction: CD8 binds to MHC Class I molecules, which present endogenous antigens, while CD4 binds to MHC Class II molecules, which present exogenous antigens. This intricate molecular binding ensures specificity and strengthens the overall T cell-target cell interaction.

Question 2
Core Principle: T cell signaling is initiated by phosphorylation of ITAM domains within the CD3 complex by Lck kinase.
Target Answer: The immediate molecular event that triggers the intracellular signaling cascade upon T cell recognition is the phosphorylation of Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Activation Motifs (ITAMs). These ITAM domains are located within the associated CD3 complex, which is non-covalently linked to the TCR. The phosphorylation of these ITAM domains is catalyzed by the Lck kinase, which is a key enzyme in initiating the downstream signaling events required for T cell activation.

Question 3
Core Principle: CTLs and Th cells differ in their MHC recognition and effector mechanisms, targeting intracellular pathogens/abnormal cells versus coordinating other immune cells, respectively.
Target Answer: Cytotoxic T Lymphocytes (CTLs/CD8+) recognize antigens presented on MHC Class I molecules, which are found on all nucleated cells and typically present endogenous antigens. Upon recognition, CTLs induce apoptosis in target cells by releasing perforin, which forms pores in the target cell membrane, and granzymes, which enter through these pores to trigger programmed cell death. In contrast, Helper T Lymphocytes (Th/CD4+) recognize antigens presented on MHC Class II molecules, primarily found on Antigen-Presenting Cells (APCs), which present exogenous antigens. Th cells do not directly kill target cells but instead secrete cytokines, which are signaling molecules that activate other immune cells such as B cells, macrophages, and CTLs, thereby coordinating the broader immune response.

Question 4
Core Principle: Inactive Lck kinase would prevent ITAM phosphorylation, thereby blocking the initiation of T cell signaling and subsequent activation.
Target Answer: If Lck kinase were rendered completely inactive by a mutation, the critical initial step of T cell signaling would be abrogated. Upon T cell receptor (TCR) recognition of a peptide-MHC complex, the associated CD3 complex's ITAM domains would not be phosphorylated. Since ITAM phosphorylation by Lck kinase is explicitly stated as the trigger for signaling cascades, the absence of this event would prevent the initiation of any downstream signaling. Consequently, T cell activation would fail, and the cellular immune response, including the differentiation and effector functions of both CTLs and Th cells, would be severely impaired or completely absent.

Question 5
Core Principle: T cell mechanisms contribute to cancer surveillance by eliminating abnormal cells and to autoimmunity when self-tolerance fails.
Target Answer: Cellular immunity contributes to cancer surveillance through Cytotoxic T Lymphocytes (CTLs). CTLs recognize and eliminate cancerous cells, which often present abnormal endogenous antigens on MHC Class I molecules, by inducing apoptosis via perforin and granzymes. This mechanism is leveraged in T cell therapies like CAR T cell therapy. Conversely, the failure to distinguish self from non-self, a process known as self-tolerance, can lead to autoimmune diseases. In conditions like Type 1 Diabetes, T cells incorrectly recognize and attack healthy host cells, demonstrating a breakdown in the crucial self/non-self discrimination that is fundamental to proper T cell function and preventing autoimmunity.
